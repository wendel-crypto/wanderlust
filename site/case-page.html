Wanderlust

A travel social app that puts Instagram-style trip sharing and TripAdvisor-style reviews in one feed. Travelers post where they went and rate it; tour operators, hotels and guides post what they offer and take bookings in the DMs.

The problem

Travel inspiration and travel trust live in different apps.

People find a place on Instagram, then leave to check it on TripAdvisor, then leave again to message the operator on WhatsApp. Each hop loses context, and the photos that inspire a trip rarely carry an honest rating.

Providers have the opposite problem: great visual content with no review layer next to it, and no direct line from a post to a booking.

Wanderlust joins the three moments, see it, trust it, book it, in one post: every photo carries a geotag, a star rating and a short review, and every provider post opens a DM with the post attached.

What's in it

Social features with a review layer built in.

01

Posts with a verdict

Every post carries a category, price level, star rating and a short review, so a pretty photo also tells you whether to go.

02

Travelers and providers

Two account types in one feed. Providers get a badge, a verified tick and a Book button; filters split the two in one tap.

03

Geotags and map

Posts are pinned to real coordinates, so the map doubles as a discovery tool and a record of where your network has been.

04

DMs with context

Messaging from a post attaches it to the thread, turning “is this available?” into a booking conversation.

05

Like, save, share

Double-tap likes, a save that feeds your trip boards, and a share sheet to friends, stories or a trip plan.

06

Reviews and ratings

A rating summary with distribution, a write-a-review flow and the latest reviews on every place.

Design system

Dark, photo-first, one warm accent.

The interface stays near-black so travel photography does the talking. A single coral-to-amber gradient marks every action (like, post, book, send), teal is reserved for providers and gold for ratings, so meaning carries by colour alone.

Type pairs Syne for display with DM Sans for UI text. Cards use 26px radii and generous padding sized for one-handed use; navigation is a floating bar with the create button in the thumb zone.

My role

Concept, UX and product direction. Built with Claude.

I defined the product (who posts, what a post must contain, how a provider gets from a photo to a booking), the screen set and the interaction rules, then iterated on the layout until it read like a native app. The prototype was written with Claude as a single HTML file with no framework or build step.

Next steps for a real launch: accounts and moderation for provider verification, a booking and payments layer, and review integrity checks.
